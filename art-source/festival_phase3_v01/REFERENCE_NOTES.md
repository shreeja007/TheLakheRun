# T024 — Architectural cues and provenance boundaries

Researched 2026-10-12. This is a fictional everyday environment concept batch, not a heritage-site reconstruction.

## Authoritative reference inspected

[UNESCO World Heritage Centre — Kathmandu Valley](https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/121/) describes the Valley's craftsmanship in brick, stone and timber. Its descriptions of fired-brick/timber structures, tiled roofs and elaborate carvings concern heritage monuments, including temples. This supports broad material context, not the authenticity of our domestic designs, proportions or gateway.

Only the web page's text was used for research. No UNESCO photograph, ornament, media file or page content was copied into the image generator or game. Media reuse rights were not verified because no media was reused. The source remains a linked research reference, not a redistribution license.

## Original proposals

The residence and shuttered shopfront use invented rectangular construction, plain timber lintels/sills, simple square grid windows and a single straight gable roof. Window/door counts, stone plinths, roof pitch, timber bands, brick color, hinges and exact joinery are modeling proposals. No claim is made that they depict an actual Kathmandu house or culturally precise construction.

The gateway is an invented ordinary opening: two brick piers and one horizontal plain timber lintel, with no ceremonial roof or ornament. It is not identified as a temple, shrine, ritual entrance or historic gateway. The stone caps/footings and connection details are proposals.

The lights are a contemporary generic six-bulb string. Bulb shape, warm glow, cable sag and eyelet mounting are original asset details, not documented festival or ritual lighting. Mount samples are visual concepts, not real-world electrical/structural installation instructions.
