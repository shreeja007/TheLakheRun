# Story, cultural grounding and review

**CONFIRMED STORY DIRECTION:** a fictional friendly trickster is pursued in a spirited, **nonviolent guardian challenge**. Optional factual/legend-based content comes in later releases. Sacred ceremonies are *not* obstacles.

## World premise — proposed, fictional

At an Indra Jatra-inspired celebration in an imaginary Kathmandu street, the beat of drums signals a playful challenge between an agile festival-goer and a guardian. The runner seeks a personal best; being caught ends the game peacefully. This is game fiction, **not** an authentic reenactment of a ritual event.

Working title: *Lakhey Run: The Guardian's Challenge* (not approved branding).

## Very short intro proposal

1. Wide view of fictional brick-lined lane and festival lighting.

3. A playful response, without ritual imitation.
4. Camera settles behind runner and player controls begin.

Goal: 8–12 seconds maximum, easily skippable, or omit entirely if it slows replay.

## What research supports (background, not automatic game content)

- Indra Jatra / Yenya is a festival of the Kathmandu Valley Newar community with masked performances, drums, chariot processions and ceremonies. Source: Nepal Tourism Board (`12_RESEARCH_SOURCES.md`).
- Local reporting describes Majipa Lakhey as having protective/guardian associations in some traditions; portrayals and oral accounts may vary.
- Reported Lakhey performances include a playful exchange with Jhyalincha. **Tuku is an independent fictional character**, not Jhyalincha, and does not copy the performer's costume/props.
- Legends of Indra's arrival and a searched-for flower/herb are told in different versions. Do not present one simplified story as universal historical fact.
- Pulu Kisi is associated with a searching elephant performance; it is *not* an unreviewed future enemy or collectible.

## Creative and cultural boundaries

- Do not portray Lakhey as a murderous horror monster.
- Do not treat the Kumari, sacred masks, temple images, or ritual chariots as crushable obstacles, score objects or enemies.
- Do not use ritual choreography or sacred sound recordings as generic effect clips without consultation/licensing.
- Historical photos are **visual references only** unless a separate license explicitly permits asset reuse.
- No claim that the game's imaginary street, dialogue, challenge, Tuku costume or actions faithfully represent festival rituals.
- Before public use, seek feedback from people knowledgeable about the particular living traditions depicted. Record what was reviewed and changes made; no review guarantees universal agreement.
